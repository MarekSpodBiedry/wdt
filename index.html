The Doomsday Method Blueprint

0. Core Concept

Every year maps an anchor weekday called its Doomsday. Weekdays translate directly to numerical indices: Sun=0, Mon=1, Tue=2, Wed=3, Thu=4, Fri=5, Sat=6.

1. Century Anchors (The 0-5-3-2 Mapping)

Century anchors follow a straightforward timeline sequence of 0, 5, 3, 2 that loops indefinitely every 400 years:

Century Anchor Day Key Value Logical Anchor Rule
1700s Sunday 0 The index starts at 0
1800s Friday 5 Steps to 5
1900s Wednesday 3 Steps to 3
2000s Tuesday 2 Current Century is locked to 2 ("Y2K Tuesday")
2100s Sunday 0 The 0-5-3-2 map loop resets completely

2. Year Contribution Calculation

Take the last two digits of the year (YY) and execute the standard algorithm:

  1. Divide YY by 12 to find a (the quotient).
  2. Find the remainder of that division, b.
  3. Divide b by 4 to get c (drop decimals).
  4. Add Century Anchor + a + b + c. Simplify this sum mod 7 to track your final localized Year Doomsday.

3. Fixed Month Anchors

These benchmark days always fall directly on Doomsday:

  • Even Months: 4/4, 6/6, 8/8, 10/10, 12/12.
  • "I work 9-to-5 at the 7-11": 5/9, 9/5, 7/11, 11/7.
  • March: "Pi Day" (March 14).
  • January / February (The Leap Year Shifters):
    • Common Year: Jan 3, Feb 28.
    • Leap Year: Jan 4, Feb 29.
Historical Calendar Shifts (Julian vs. Gregorian) The code's validation pipeline tracks the exact date boundaries where calendar lines fractured. To mentally calculate these target blocks, apply these region-specific manual adjustments:

• The Standard 1582 Cutoff (Catholic Europe):
The Julian calendar ended on Thursday, October 4, 1582. The next morning skipped 10 days forward directly into the Gregorian calendar on Friday, October 15, 1582. Calculation step: For any year before this break, every single year divisible by 4 is a leap year (including 1500, 1400, etc.). The century anchor value decreases by 1 day index every 100 years you move backward.

• The 1752 Split (British Empire & American Colonies):
Great Britain and its global territories maintained the Julian system until Wednesday, September 2, 1752. The following dawn skipped 11 calendar dates to reset on Thursday, September 14, 1752. Calculation step: Dates falling between October 15, 1582, and September 2, 1752, are calculated using old-style Julian leap rules if simulating British records. To sync a Julian date to the 0-5-3-2 Gregorian loop matrix, add the calendar gap manually to your target date index (+10 days in the 1600s; +11 days in the 1700s) before reducing mod 7.
